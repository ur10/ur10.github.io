I am a Master's student in Robotics at Northeastern University, working in the Helping Hands Lab on memory and partial observability in learned manipulation policies, and on certifiable optimization for state estimation. My work spans imitation learning, diffusion policies, offline reinforcement learning, sequence modeling, and convex relaxations for factor-graph estimation.

I am interested in two questions: how to make policies reason over history rather than the current frame alone, and how to make an estimator return a certificate that its solution is globally optimal rather than a hope that the local solver landed in the right basin. Before Northeastern I spent two years at Mowito deploying 50 Hz MPC trajectory generation in C++ on UR5 hardware for warehouse picking, and I have published on driving-scene perception at IROS 2024 and multi-robot coordination in IEEE RA-L.

Research

Memory & Partial Observability
History-conditioned sequence models, long-context transformers and recurrent memory, handle tasks where the current observation is not sufficient to act. Each environment ships with a fully observable twin, so any gap in success rate is attributable to observability rather than to task difficulty, and the tasks are built to force a policy to carry information across a long horizon before it can act correctly.
Long-Context Policies & Latency-Aware Inference
A long history is only useful if it fits inside a control budget. Action chunking and caching keep inference within the control loop, while memory-efficient attention such as FlashAttention and xFormers reduces rollout memory two to three times. Together these let long-context models execute in real time instead of exhausting GPU memory partway through an episode. Latency, not accuracy, is usually what keeps such a policy off the robot.
3D Scene Representations for Planning
Efficient 3D scene representations that distill Gaussian Splatting reconstructions into point clouds, preserving the geometric detail a manipulator needs for contact and clearance while staying cheap enough to query inside a control loop. The representation is built for fast online motion evaluation in environments that change while the robot is acting, rather than being reconstructed once and then assumed static for the rest of the episode.
Certifiable State Estimation
Convex relaxations of factor-graph maximum likelihood estimation, lifting the problem to a quadratically constrained quadratic program and then to a semidefinite program whose solution carries a dual certificate of global optimality. Where a local solver can converge to a bad minimum and report nothing unusual, a rank-one certificate proves the recovered estimate is the global optimum, and a nonzero gap bounds how far from it you are.

Selected Projects

History-Conditioned Policies under Partial Observability
A policy conditioned only on the current observation is effectively Markov, yet many manipulation tasks hide exactly the information it needs: a route specified before the robot sets off, or objects already handled and no longer in view. Conditioning on a history of observations, through long-context transformers and recurrent memory, carries that information forward. Route following and object counting serve as the test tasks, each paired with a fully observable twin so a drop in success isolates observability from difficulty.
Environments / Code
3D Scene Representations for Online Motion Planning
Planning in a changing scene needs a representation that is geometrically faithful and cheap enough to query inside a control loop, and those requirements pull against each other: photorealistic reconstructions are expensive to evaluate, while coarse occupancy grids discard the geometry a manipulator needs. Gaussian Splatting reconstructions are distilled into point clouds that retain the detail mattering for contact and clearance, while dense point tracking keeps the representation aligned with objects as they move.
Fine-Grained Point-Cloud Manipulation
Sim-trained point-cloud policies generalize well across objects and poses, but real depth breaks their assumptions: holes, occlusion, calibration drift, and contact mismatch. The sim-trained 3D policy stays frozen so its geometry prior survives, while a small off-policy residual learns on top of it. A gate decides when that correction is trustworthy, and the correction itself stays bounded for safety. Evaluation targets the precise-contact tasks where 3D policies are weakest, insertion above all.
