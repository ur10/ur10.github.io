I am a Master's student in Robotics at Northeastern University, working in the Helping Hands Lab on memory and partial observability in learned manipulation policies, and on certifiable optimization for state estimation. My work spans imitation learning, diffusion policies, offline reinforcement learning, sequence modeling, and convex relaxations for factor-graph estimation.

I am interested in two questions: how to make policies reason over history rather than the current frame alone, and how to make an estimator return a certificate that its solution is globally optimal rather than a hope that the local solver landed in the right basin. Before Northeastern I spent two years at Mowito deploying 50 Hz MPC trajectory generation in C++ on UR5 hardware for warehouse picking, and I have published on driving-scene perception at IROS 2024 and multi-robot coordination in IEEE RA-L.

Research

Memory & Partial Observability
History-conditioned sequence models, long-context transformers and recurrent memory, for temporal prediction in tasks where the current observation is not sufficient to act. I build the benchmarks alongside the models: environments that ship with a fully observable twin, so any performance gap is attributable to observability rather than to task difficulty, and tasks that force a policy to carry information forward across a long horizon before it can act correctly.
Long-Context Policies & Latency-Aware Inference
A long history is only useful if it fits inside a control budget. I work on making long-context policies deployable on real hardware through action chunking and caching, which keep inference inside the control loop, and memory-efficient attention such as FlashAttention and xFormers, which reduces rollout memory two to three times. Together these let long-context models execute in real time instead of exhausting GPU memory partway through an episode.
3D Scene Representations for Planning
Efficient 3D scene representations that distill Gaussian Splatting reconstructions into point clouds, preserving the geometric detail a manipulator needs for contact and clearance while staying cheap enough to query inside a control loop. The representation is built for fast online motion evaluation in environments that change while the robot is acting, rather than being reconstructed once and then assumed static for the rest of the episode.
Diffusion Policies & Multimodal Trajectory Generation
Conditional diffusion policies for multimodal trajectory generation, conditioned on RGB-D and 3D perception together with proprioception. This includes multimodal input pipelines that fuse point-cloud and proprioceptive features through cross-attention transformers and 3D encoders, so a policy can represent several valid ways of completing a task instead of averaging them into a single trajectory that satisfies none of them. Most manipulation tasks admit more than one correct solution.
Offline RL & Sim-to-Real Transfer
Learning policies from logged demonstration data with offline reinforcement learning, IQL and CQL, and behavior-regularized methods such as TD3+BC that stay robust when demonstrations are limited. On the transfer side, procedural simulation with visual and dynamics domain randomization to generate synthetic training data, with the resulting generalization gains validated through side-by-side hardware A/B trials rather than in simulation alone. The aim is a policy that survives the gap between the distribution it was trained on and the one it meets on the robot.
Certifiable State Estimation
Convex relaxations of factor-graph maximum likelihood estimation, lifting the problem to a quadratically constrained quadratic program and then to a semidefinite program whose solution carries a dual certificate of global optimality. Where a local solver can converge to a bad minimum and report nothing unusual, a rank-one certificate proves the recovered estimate is the global optimum, and a nonzero gap bounds how far from it you are.

Selected Projects

History-Conditioned Policies under Partial Observability
Helping Hands Lab, Northeastern University. A policy conditioned only on the current observation is effectively Markov, yet many manipulation tasks hide exactly the information it needs: a route specified before the robot sets off, or objects already handled and no longer in view. I implement history-conditioned sequence models for temporal prediction under partial observability and build the tasks that test them, including route following and a counting task where the robot must track how many objects it has already handled.
Environments / Code
3D Scene Representations for Online Motion Planning
Helping Hands Lab, Northeastern University. Planning in a dynamic environment needs a scene representation that is geometrically faithful and cheap enough to query inside a control loop, and those requirements pull against each other: photorealistic reconstructions are expensive to evaluate, while coarse occupancy grids discard the geometry a manipulator needs. I distill Gaussian Splatting reconstructions into point clouds that preserve the detail mattering for contact and clearance while remaining fast enough to score motions online as the scene changes.
