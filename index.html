I work at the intersection of robot learning, control, and optimization, with a focus on building autonomous systems that are reliable on real hardware and whose behavior can be rigorously verified.

I’m a Graduate Researcher at Northeastern University’s Helping Hands and NEURAL Labs. My work combines learning-based control for robotic manipulation, including diffusion policies and reinforcement learning, with optimization-based methods such as MPC and real-time QP safety filters to enforce physical constraints. In parallel, I research certifiable state estimation using semidefinite optimization, developing methods that go beyond locally optimal solutions to provide verifiable optimality guarantees.

I’m particularly interested in bringing together the adaptability of learned policies and the mathematical rigor of classical control and optimization to build more capable, dependable robotic systems.

Real-to-Sim Robot Learning with a Gaussian-Splat Twin

Real-world data · phone scan, 2× RealSense, rendered robot

  • Real2Sim
  • 3D reconstruction
  • Gaussian splatting
  • Digital twin
  • Synthetic data generation
  • Domain randomization
  • Imitation learning
  • Policy training
  • Trajectory optimization
  • Cross-embodiment

I build a calibrated digital twin of a real UR5e workspace to generate training data for sim-to-real robot learning. A Gaussian-splat reconstruction captures the bench; two calibrated cameras recover metric geometry and object motion. One human demonstration per task becomes varied robot demonstrations in simulation.

The central challenges are recovering metric scale, tracking small objects through occlusion, and generating motions the robot can execute. I address these through geometric registration, object tracking, robot integration, and trajectory repair around new obstacles. The pipeline generates demonstrations for cube transfer, hanging a tape roll, and placing a nut on a bolt.

On the cube task, 400 generated demonstrations raised policy success from 3% to 94% on held-out clean scenes. Training with repaired obstacle demonstrations achieved 85% on blocked routes. These results come from closed-loop evaluations in the rendered twin.

Adapting a 3D Policy for Precise Robot Insertion

Real robot · UR10

  • Residual RL
  • Off-policy RL (SAC)
  • Sim2Real
  • Point-cloud policies
  • Diffusion policy
  • Policy adaptation
  • Bounded corrections

A robot can approach a peg correctly and still miss the final insertion. This project studies how to adapt a simulation-trained 3D policy for precise ring-on-peg manipulation on a physical UR10, where missing depth points, occlusion, and calibration errors make alignment harder.

I worked on a residual controller that learns small corrections while the pretrained policy stays fixed. Two RealSense cameras supply the point cloud; a learned gate selects when to apply a correction, and explicit limits bound its size. The aim is to improve alignment while retaining the base policy’s learned behavior.

The videos show example runs with and without the residual on the real robot. The public repository describes the point-cloud processing, residual learning, and action-selection components.

3D Scene Representations for Online Motion Planning

Real-world data · tracked on real scenes

  • 3D reconstruction
  • Gaussian splatting
  • Point clouds
  • Dense point tracking
  • Motion planning
  • Collision checking

This project explores how to represent changing 3D environments in a form that’s useful for robot planning. I used Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct real tabletop scenes and dense point tracking to follow objects as they move. The reconstructed scenes are converted into point clouds that are easier to work with for geometric queries, with the longer-term goal of supporting motion planning in dynamic environments. It uses the same kind of Gaussian-splat reconstruction as the digital twin above, here as a planning representation rather than for rendering training data.

History-Conditioned Policies under Partial Observability

Real robot · UR5

  • Imitation learning
  • Diffusion policy
  • Policy training
  • Partial observability (POMDP)
  • Memory
  • Transformers
  • Recurrent policies
  • Benchmark design

Some robot tasks require remembering information that is no longer visible in the current camera view. I studied how recurrent policies and long-context transformers handle these situations, using route-following and object-counting tasks where the robot has to remember earlier observations to act correctly. Each task also has a fully observable version, allowing us to compare performance with and without the need for memory. The counting task is also demonstrated on a physical UR5.

Collision-Aware Soft-Capture Trajectory Optimization on SE(3)

  • Trajectory optimization
  • Lie groups / SE(3)
  • Rigid-body dynamics
  • Collision avoidance
  • Space robotics

Docking with a tumbling spacecraft requires planning both position and orientation while respecting collision and sensing constraints. I formulated this as a trajectory optimization problem directly on SE(3), using left-trivialized rigid-body dynamics to capture the coupled motion. The optimization incorporates keep-out regions, collision clearance, and sensing limits through differentiable penalties, with collision terms linearized in local Lie-algebra coordinates. This allows the motion and geometric constraints to be handled within the same manifold-based optimization framework.

Research

Memory & Partial Observability

History-conditioned sequence models, long-context transformers and recurrent memory, handle tasks where the current observation is not sufficient to act. Each environment ships with a fully observable twin, so any gap in success rate is attributable to observability rather than to task difficulty, and the tasks are built to force a policy to carry information across a long horizon before it can act correctly.

Long-Context Policies & Latency-Aware Inference

A long history is only useful if it fits inside a control budget. Action chunking and caching keep inference within the control loop, while memory-efficient attention such as FlashAttention and xFormers reduces rollout memory two to three times. Together these let long-context models execute in real time instead of exhausting GPU memory partway through an episode. Latency, not accuracy, is usually what keeps such a policy off the robot.

3D Scene Representations for Planning

Efficient 3D scene representations that distill Gaussian Splatting reconstructions into point clouds, preserving the geometric detail a manipulator needs for contact and clearance while staying cheap enough to query inside a control loop. The representation is built for fast online motion evaluation in environments that change while the robot is acting, rather than being reconstructed once and then assumed static for the rest of the episode.

Certifiable State Estimation

Convex relaxations of factor-graph maximum likelihood estimation, lifting the problem to a quadratically constrained quadratic program and then to a semidefinite program whose solution carries a dual certificate of global optimality. Where a local solver can converge to a bad minimum and report nothing unusual, a rank-one certificate proves the recovered estimate is the global optimum, and a nonzero gap bounds how far from it you are.
